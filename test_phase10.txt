1. THE PROBLEM THIS PHASE POSES
-------------------------------

Every other phase answers a question the user asked. This one SPEAKS WITHOUT
BEING ASKED, and quantifies a person's life while doing it. That makes it the
phase where fabrication would do the most damage and be hardest to notice.

Three rules govern every function, and each is tested:

  1. NO CLAIM WITHOUT EVIDENCE. Every finding carries the numbers it came
     from, and the UI shows them beside the claim.
  2. NEVER DIAGNOSE. Life OS reports what happened — "moved four times" —
     never why. It has counts, not insight into a person.
  3. INSUFFICIENT DATA MEANS SILENCE. Below a minimum sample the answer is
     `null`, not a guess dressed as an observation.

Most of this phase's tests are NEGATIVE assertions: proof that the system
stays quiet when it does not know something.

4. THE DEFECT THIS PHASE'S TESTS CAUGHT
---------------------------------------

**A BRAND-NEW ACCOUNT WAS SCORED 0 OUT OF 100.**

The wellbeing component computed `checkInCount / daysPossible`. For a user who
signed up an hour ago that is 0/28 = 0%, which was then the ONLY measured
component, so the overall life score came out as 0 — and the Insights page
would have greeted a new user with a quantified verdict that their life rates
zero.

This is exactly the class of bug this phase is most at risk of: arithmetically
defensible, superficially reasonable, and genuinely harmful.

Fixed: never having logged a check-in is NOT TRACKED, precisely like never
having scheduled a habit. `score` is `null` until there is at least one
check-in, after which the ratio is measured normally. Caught by an integration
test written specifically to assert a new account is told nothing.


5. RESTRAINT IN THE PROACTIVE SCAN
----------------------------------

  5.1 AT MOST ONCE PER LOCAL DAY per profile
      Verified: a second scan one minute later creates nothing and reports the
      profile as skipped. The check is against notifications actually written,
      so it survives a worker restart.

  5.2 AT MOST 3 INSIGHTS, most severe first
      A system that surfaces everything it notices is noise, and noise gets
      muted. Tested with ten candidates.

  5.3 ONLY INSIDE WAKING HOURS (08:00–21:00 local)
      Verified: a scan at 03:00 local examines nobody.

  5.4 PROACTIVE ALERTS NEVER OVERRIDE QUIET HOURS
      This is the single most important assertion in the phase. Phase 6 lets
      urgent notifications bypass quiet hours; proactive ones explicitly pass
      `isUrgent: false`. Verified with quiet hours set to the entire day: the
      insight is RECORDED but NOT delivered, and stays PENDING.

      The reasoning, stated in the code: the user did not ask for it, so it
      does not get to override their silence.

  5.5 Honours a disabled PROACTIVE channel. Verified.

  5.6 Nothing to say means nothing sent. Verified: a scan over accounts with
      no findings creates zero notifications.

  5.7 Failures are isolated per profile — one user's bad data cannot stop
      everyone else's scan.

LIVE VERIFICATION: the scan was executed through the REAL worker entrypoint
(`pnpm worker:once`, which calls the same `proactiveScan()` the queued job
calls) against the real database, returning honest zeroes on an empty system.
The hourly `proactive-scan` job is registered in `scripts/worker.ts` alongside
the reminder sweep verified live in Phase 6.


6. THE THRESHOLDS, AND WHY EACH IS WHERE IT IS
----------------------------------------------

  FORGOTTEN_DAYS = 30
    A task untouched for a week is normal life. Setting this low turns the
    feature into a nagging machine, which users disable — and a disabled
    feature helps nobody. Items WITH a due date are never "forgotten"; they
    are scheduled.

  MIN_PATTERN_SAMPLE = 20, MIN_PATTERN_MARGIN = 3
    "You're most productive on Tuesdays" drawn from three data points is
    astrology. Two days differing by one completion is noise, and reporting it
    would train the user to ignore everything this module says. Both floors
    are tested by asserting SILENCE below them.

  MIN_TREND_CHANGE = 15 percentage points
    A 62%-vs-58% change is not a trend.

  MAX_PROACTIVE_PER_DAY = 3
    See §5.2.

Every threshold is a named constant with the reasoning in a comment beside it,
so a future change is a deliberate decision rather than a tweak.


7. HONESTY IN THE NUMBERS
-------------------------

  7.1 The life score averages ONLY measured components, and says how many
      ("Averaged across 2 of 4 areas — the rest have nothing to measure").
      With no components at all it is `null` and explains itself. There is no
      default 50 "to have something to show".

  7.2 Deadline risk uses TODAY'S ACTUAL FREE TIME, not the theoretical
      working-hours window. A student whose days are full of classes does not
      have eight free hours, and calling a deadline "comfortable" on that basis
      would be a confident wrong answer. (This was found when lint flagged the
      free-time query as unused — the real defect was larger than the lint.)

  7.3 IMPOSSIBLE is said, not softened. 50 hours of work in 2 days is reported
      as not achievable, with the arithmetic shown.

  7.4 The time audit reports ONLY logged time and says so. An empty chart
      implying "you did nothing" would be a lie; the user simply did not log.

  7.5 Patterns are phrased as observation, never diagnosis. The UI section is
      titled "Patterns" with "Observed from your history. What happened, not
      why."


8. CROSS-USER ISOLATION
-----------------------

Verified inside the intelligence layer, which reads from eight other modules
at once and is therefore where a leak could most easily hide:

  - Another user's 90-day-stale task does not appear, and the serialised
    report does not contain its title anywhere.
  - Another user's 30 completions do not create this user's patterns.
  - The life score is computed per profile — a data-rich neighbour does not
    give this user a score.
  - Each profile receives only its own proactive notifications.
