1. THE ARCHITECTURAL DECISION
-----------------------------

**PLANNING DOES NOT USE A MODEL.**

A daily plan is scheduling arithmetic — fit these items into those gaps,
respecting priority, energy and deadlines — not a language problem. Building
it as arithmetic buys three things a generated plan cannot have:

  1. It is DETERMINISTIC. The same inputs always give the same plan, so it can
     be tested, debugged, and trusted not to shuffle between refreshes.
  2. It WORKS WITHOUT AN API KEY. A student whose quota runs out mid-semester
     does not lose the ability to plan their day.
  3. It is VERIFIABLE. Every figure on the Plan page is a sum the user could
     do themselves, and every block carries the reason it landed there.

Phase 8's AI layer may later PHRASE a plan. It does not compute one.

This is also why Phase 9 is the first phase since Phase 3 with NO BLOCKED
VERIFICATION: there is no external dependency to be blocked on.

4. THE SCHEDULING RULES, AND WHY
--------------------------------

4.1 Priority order comes from the EXISTING engine
  The planner does not re-decide what matters; it takes the order Phase 2's
  priority engine already produced. One source of truth for "what is most
  important", not two that can disagree.

4.2 It never books over a real commitment
  Slots come from Phase 6's free-time engine, which already excludes classes,
  exams and events. VERIFIED AGAINST THE DATABASE: a 09:00–12:00 lecture
  moves the first block to 12:00 and drops available time from 480 to 300
  minutes.

4.3 A deadline does not consume planning time
  Due dates are moments, not blocks (Phase 6's `isBusy: false`). Verified: a
  task due at 15:00 still leaves the full 480 minutes free to schedule in.

4.4 HIGH-energy work prefers peak hours — but is still scheduled
  If no peak slot is free it lands anyway, because an unscheduled important
  task helps nobody. The REASON records which happened, and the UI shows it.

4.5 Blocks are capped at 90 minutes with a 10-minute gap
  Nobody does three hours of one thing. Long items are SPLIT across slots
  rather than dropped, and never into fragments under 15 minutes — a
  seven-minute fragment is interruption, not work.

4.6 Anything that does not fit is returned WITH A REASON
  `unplanned` is part of the output, not a silent omission. Silently dropping
  work would make the plan a lie. Tested for both "no free time at all" and
  "no gap long enough left".

4.7 Deterministic and non-mutating
  Tested explicitly: identical inputs produce identical plans, and the
  caller's array is never sorted in place.


5. HONESTY IN THE INTELLIGENCE
------------------------------

This phase had the most opportunities to produce comforting nonsense. Each was
refused, and each refusal is tested.

5.1 OVERCOMMITTED is said plainly
  A 12-hour day in an 8-hour window reports "Overcommitted — today needs more
  hours than it has. Something will have to move." Not "ambitious", not
  "a full day ahead!".

5.2 Recovery mode NAMES what to drop
  A plan that only says "prioritise" leaves the hardest decision with the
  person who is already overwhelmed. `buildRecoveryPlan` returns an explicit
  `focus` list and an explicit `defer` list, and promises only as many focus
  items as the day can actually hold. Tested that every item lands in one list
  or the other — nothing silently disappears.

5.3 Exam mode warns instead of recommending an impossible day
  40 topics in 2 days is 600 minutes a day. The engine computes that honestly
  and then says "more than you have — pick the topics that matter most",
  rather than presenting a ten-hour schedule as advice.

5.4 Procrastination detection is conservative and descriptive
  One reschedule is a person with a changing day, not a problem. The signal
  fires at THREE reschedules, or two on something older than a fortnight —
  patterns rather than moments. Counts come from the ACTIVITY LOG, the record
  of what actually happened, not a counter that could drift.

  The UI heading is "Keeps getting pushed" with the subtitle "What happened,
  not why. Only you know that." The system reports behaviour; it does not
  diagnose motivation.

5.5 Weekly observations are facts, not prose
  Each observation is generated from a THRESHOLD on a real figure and contains
  the figure. "Less than half of what was due got done (25%)." A quiet week
  says "A steady week, with nothing unusual in the numbers" rather than
  manufacturing an insight.

5.6 Null is not zero
  A week with nothing due reports `completionRate: null` and renders "Nothing
  was due" — not 0%. Same for habit consistency with no scheduled habits.

5.7 An empty day says so
  No tasks due means `isEmpty: true` and "Nothing to plan… That is a real
  answer, not an empty screen." No sample blocks, ever.


6. CROSS-USER ISOLATION
-----------------------

Verified inside the planning layer specifically, because a planner reads from
six other modules at once and is exactly where a leak could hide:

  - Another user's URGENT task does not appear in this user's plan.
  - Another user's all-day busy event does not reduce this user's free time
    (asserted numerically: still 480 minutes).
  - Another user's reschedules do not raise this user's procrastination
    signals — and do correctly raise their own.
  - Another user's imminent exam does not activate this user's exam mode.
  - Another user's task creation does not appear in this user's weekly review.


7. DEFECTS FOUND AND FIXED DURING THIS PHASE
--------------------------------------------

  1. `DailyPlan["unplanned"]` was typed readonly and then pushed to. Caught by
     typecheck; fixed by building a mutable local and returning it as the
     readonly type, rather than casting the readonly away.

  2. A test used `sortOrder` on ExamTopic, which is `position`. Caught by the
     integration run against the real schema — the kind of mistake that only a
     real database catches.

     behaviour. Inferring it would need months of data Life OS does not yet
     have, and guessing would be worse than using what the user told us.
